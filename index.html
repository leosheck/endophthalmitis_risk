Endophthalmitis Risk

How often endophthalmitis follows an intravitreal injection or a cataract operation, pooled from published series, and how much each preventive measure or risk factor changes it. Every interval is a 95% confidence interval. Numbers in brackets point to the numbered sources at the foot of the page.

Factors that change the risk

Ratios above 1 mean more infections with the named factor; below 1, fewer. Squares are single studies (counts as infections over injections), hollow diamonds are published meta-analyses, and the filled diamond is a random-effects pool of the single studies calculated for this page. Relative risk here means the risk ratio calculated from counts or, where a paper gave only an adjusted estimate, its published odds ratio or incidence rate ratio (the measure is named when a row is hovered). For an outcome this rare these measures are numerically almost identical. No included study reported a hazard ratio. The axis is logarithmic so that halving and doubling sit the same distance from 1.

Search and methods

Sources searched, in order

  • Bear's journal reading wiki (endophthalmitis page, about 41 sources, and the cataract, anti-VEGF and steroid pages), then the Zotero library (66 items mentioning endophthalmitis, plus a full-text search of the stored PDFs). Items held there are marked Library in the list of sources.
  • PubMed, searched on 8 October 2026: 69 queries plus related-article searches from key papers. 827 abstracts were screened and PubMed Central full text was opened where an abstract lacked counts. The full query list and exclusions are in the search log kept with the page source.
  • Not found or not available: the full ESCRS 2007 paper (abstract and the 2006 interim counts used), the NZ national audit of injection or cataract endophthalmitis, and any study of needle gauge or lid speculum with counts.

Calculations

  • Rate intervals are exact (Clopper-Pearson) binomial intervals from the reported counts.
  • Pooled rates: DerSimonian-Laird random effects on the logit scale, 0.5 added to zero counts. The prediction interval shows where a new centre's rate is expected to fall.
  • Ratios from counts: risk ratio with a log-normal interval, 0.5 added to every cell when a cell is zero. Reported odds ratios, incidence rate ratios and adjusted estimates are shown as published (inverted where the paper used the opposite reference). For an event this rare, odds and risk ratios are numerically almost identical.
  • Pooled ratios combine single-study rows only (never meta-analyses), with DerSimonian-Laird weights; I² is given with each pool. Rows marked "not pooled" share a reference group with another row.
  • Counts marked "derived" were back-calculated from a reported rate and denominator.

Read with care

  • Case definitions differ: some series count only culture-proven or tapped-and-injected cases, others count any diagnosis code, which includes sterile inflammation. This alone can explain a several-fold spread between centres.
  • Almost all comparisons are observational, and many are before-and-after changes in one unit, where other practices changed at the same time.
